Method & data coverage

The city view combines mapped terrain, buildings, transport links, trees and street assets from the checked-in scene products. Building outlines prefer OpenStreetMap footprints and mapped building parts, with municipal photogrammetry footprints filling gaps; available heights use aligned survey records, LiDAR samples or mapped tags according to coverage.

Street fixtures, crossings, parking and public lights follow municipal records where supplied. Some displayed dimensions and pole heights are inferred for visualisation, not surveyed. Weather and traffic context may use external modelled or observed feeds when available; each panel labels its source and freshness.

Layer visibility is not a completeness check. An unmapped feature, missing tag or blank area means the selected source has no usable record there; it does not confirm that the real-world feature is absent.

      Method & limitations

      Near-live UTCI and mean radiant temperature (Tmrt) combine weather inputs with mapped urban geometry and surface response. Forecast and shade-intervention results are experimental model estimates for the selected frame, not street measurements. Missing or stale thermal coverage is reported separately from the map and should not be read as comfortable conditions.

      The historical profile uses ERA5 reanalysis composites for 2016–2025. Weather is averaged by month and South African local hour before the local surface and shade model is run; the result represents a composite climate condition, not a reconstructed day or the average of hourly UTCI observations.

      Satellite-era surface-temperature layers are a separate screening product from UTCI. Shade-priority and shade-deficit views combine their fixed summer baseline with the selected date and time and mapped building/canopy shadows. They do not estimate a new UTCI value for that date. Results cover valid mapped cells only.

          Method & limitations

          Shadow views calculate the sun position for Cape Town at the chosen local date and time, then project clear-sky direct-light geometry against mapped buildings and tree canopies. The diagram and map show geometric sun and shade; cloud cover, diffuse sky light and reflected light are not part of this calculation.

          Sun-hours studies sample direct sunlight across the selected time window and add the sunlit time for each selected ground, roof or façade surface. The study area, time step and surface resolution trade detail for calculation time. Use the same area, surfaces and time settings when comparing dates or design options. The result is not total daylight duration or solar-energy yield.

          These are exploratory modelled comparisons, not measured radiation or engineering-grade solar analysis. Building massing and roof surfaces are simplified from mapped footprints and height sources; detailed roof obstructions and small structures may be missing.

          How to read this · method and limits

          The overall pollutant screen combines modelled road NOx and exhaust PMx emissions as a single mass-weighted tracer, then maps relative near-ground residence through one steady OpenFOAM wind field. Its random walk assumes locally isotropic turbulence inferred from k and epsilon, with an isotropic Gaussian well-mixed correction; it does not use a full Reynolds-stress turbulence model and has not been validated against pollutant measurements. The heat map shows where the tracer persists in the model, not a resolved concentration or measured air quality. Blank areas may have no recorded pollutant residence or only trace below the display cutoff; neither establishes clean air. Demand is synthetic unless observed edge counts are configured. This exploratory screen does not predict health risk or the effect of planting trees.

              Method & limitations

              Direction view displays the available full-CBD steady RANS k–ε OpenFOAM case for the selected wind direction. Pedestrian speed is sampled from the CFD volume at the selected terrain-following height; vertical velocity, pressure and turbulence are solved around the mapped buildings. A direction is selectable only when its converted case is available.

              The animated heat map transports speed colours through the horizontal CFD velocity field, with local turbulence energy shaping the colour variation. These moving colours are visual tracers and do not solve changing wind speeds. Turn motion off to read the mean-speed map; inspected values and statistics always use the solved mean field. In 3D volume mode, flowlines sample the selected flow box through its configured height. Comfort combines available solved directions using the ERA5 wind rose and Weibull exceedance. Missing sectors are excluded rather than interpolated or treated as calm.

              These CFD fields are exploratory and unvalidated. They have not passed domain and mesh sensitivity or local field-validation gates, so they are not suitable for certification. The steady inlet does not simulate changing atmospheric stability or transient turbulence.

              Pollutant dispersal combines modelled road NOx and exhaust PMx as a mass-weighted tracer, then estimates near-ground residence through one steady wind field with a random walk using turbulence inferred from k and epsilon. Colours are relative residence within the scenario, not measured concentration or health risk; synthetic traffic demand and isotropic turbulence assumptions limit interpretation.

              Method & limitations

              This is a screening estimate of mapped line-of-sight visibility from one observer point at the selected eye height. Use Pick viewpoint on map to place or move the observer; Alt-click can target terrain below buildings. For each ground sample within the selected radius and viewing shape, it tests the sightline against mapped terrain triangles and mapped building base-to-top massing. Heading and horizontal spread set the cone direction and width; full 360° removes the directional limit.

              Green ground is visible from the observer and red ground is blocked by terrain or a mapped building. White trees and street details provide scene context but do not block the line-of-sight calculation. Small structures, unmapped objects, sub-grid terrain detail and atmospheric refraction are not represented. Visible share is the percent of valid sampled ground cells with a clear sightline.

              Sources, coverage & limitations

              Loading source coverage…

              MyCiTi source tables are weekday only. Their stop times are interpolated from trip endpoints; some route directions use an estimated cadence. Weekend service days are excluded because weekend schedules are not supplied. Windows crossing midnight use each calendar service day's schedule. Public holidays use the weekday table because no holiday calendar is modelled.

              PRASA timetable times are used where matched; unmatched corridors use an estimated cadence. Windows crossing midnight use the schedule for each calendar service day. Movement counts are departures at nearby stops, not direction-aware end-to-end trips. No live vehicle positions, disruptions, transfers, fares, minibus-taxi services, passenger counts or accessibility assessment are included.

              Nominal places per departure (60 per bus, 800 per train) are rough assumptions. They do not measure spare seats or operator capacity. Treat possible extra departures as questions for the operator, not service recommendations.

              Method & limitations

              Routes use the checked-in CBD OpenStreetMap pedestrian network. Explicitly prohibited ways and motorways are filtered; each endpoint snaps to its nearest directed walkable segment and picks more than 50 m away are rejected. Segments without foot-access, pedestrian-way or sidewalk evidence remain available but are flagged as uncertain. Mapped steps are included.

              The tool compares fastest estimated walking time, least direct-sun time and lowest cumulative UTCI heat load when coverage allows. Walking time assumes 4.8 km/h. Shade is sampled along each route in five-minute bins; UTCI and pedestrian wind use the nearest complete hourly forecast frame within 30 minutes. Missing or stale thermal data removes the heat-ranked alternative while leaving other routes available.

              Each route has a matched cohort of 100 synthetic walkers with seeded speeds from 4.0–5.6 km/h, using the same speed for each person across alternatives. Walkers are not a demographic or crowd model. Street evidence reports mapped crossings, lighting, sidewalk and width tags; missing tags remain unknown. Forecasts are not street observations, and the results do not establish safety or wheelchair accessibility.
