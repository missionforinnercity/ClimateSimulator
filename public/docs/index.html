Using results responsibly

How the model works

Climate Explorer brings environmental and mobility evidence into one interactive city model. This guide explains how to run each tool, read its outputs and turn the results into informed placemaking priorities. Jump to a tool from the navigation or open it directly in the explorer.

Map tools & current conditions

Reference

Control the shared scene, inspect street context, and apply current modelled weather to the sunlight and wind tools.

Scene controls

Toggle ground, water, grass, railways, paths, roads, buildings and trees independently. Buildings can use a neutral material or an elevation colour ramp. Use Fit to CBD to restore the full model view.

Navigation

Left-drag orbits, right-drag or Shift-drag pans, the wheel zooms, and double-click resets the camera. Layer choices are retained while analysis modes temporarily simplify the scene.

Street View

Select Drop pin, click mapped terrain, then follow the generated Google Maps link. The pin is converted from viewer-local metres to WGS84; imagery availability is controlled by Google.

Current conditions

The API normalises Open-Meteo air temperature, apparent temperature, humidity, 10 m wind speed and direction. Use current applies local time to sunlight and the 10 m wind as forcing for the exploratory pedestrian wind view.

Current-conditions context. Weather values come from Open-Meteo's modelled conditions and are refreshed on demand. They update sunlight timing and wind forcing; the dedicated summer heat baseline remains a consistent comparison layer.

Urban heat

Planning analysis

Surface-temperature evidence and shade-deficit modelling to prioritise cooling interventions across the CBD.

Quick workflow
  1. Open Heat and turn on Show.
  2. Choose the question you want to ask from Layer.
  3. For shade-sensitive layers, set the scenario date and time.
  4. Read the map with the area-weighted average, priority area and maximum summary.
Controls and outputs
ControlMeaning
Intervention priority70% normalised summer surface-temperature baseline plus 30% shade deficit for the selected date and time.
Thermal exposureA comparative pedestrian heat-exposure indicator for identifying higher- and lower-priority areas.
Shade deficitScores mapped zones from more shaded to more exposed using current sun geometry.
Surface / rooftop temperatureShows the fixed source LST model on ground zones or painted onto mapped roof surfaces.
SummaryArea-weighted average, top-priority area, and maximum value for the visible metric.
What it shows

Defaults to an intervention-priority view: a fixed Summer 2025–26 surface-temperature baseline (70% weight) combined with a shade deficit computed for the selected date and time (30% weight). Because the temperature term doesn't vary by date, this is labelled a "summer thermal baseline + selected-date shade scenario" — a winter date pairs winter shade geometry with the summer baseline temperature, not a winter temperature. Users can switch to pedestrian thermal-exposure proxy, shade deficit alone, raw surface temperature, or a rooftop-temperature view clipped to building footprints.

Data & method
  • Uses a continuous Summer 2025–26 land-surface-temperature baseline aligned to the city model.
  • Combines temperature evidence with date- and time-specific shade geometry from mapped buildings and canopies.
  • Rooftop values place the source temperature model onto detailed roof geometry for consistent spatial comparison.
Best used for prioritisation. Use the relative patterns to identify places for shade, planting, surface upgrades and more detailed site assessment. The exposure layer is a comparative indicator rather than a human thermal-comfort index.

Sunlight & shadows

Planning analysis

Instant GPU shadow casts alongside a CPU ray-traced cumulative sun-hours study on ground, roofs and façades.

Quick workflow
  1. Choose Shadows for one moment or Sun hours for a time window.
  2. Set the date and either a time or start/end window.
  3. For Sun hours, choose surfaces, grid resolution and analysis-area size, then move the box if needed.
  4. Generate the study and interpret the least-to-most-sun legend.
Controls and outputs
ControlMeaning
ShadowsInstant terrain-aware GPU shadow display for the selected date and ten-minute time increment.
Analysis surfacesGround plus buildings, building roofs/façades only, or ground only.
Time step15, 30 or 60 minutes. Smaller steps capture change more finely but require more rays.
Building grid10 m for faster studies or 5 m for smoother roof and façade cells.
Analysis areaA movable 300, 500 or 750 m local domain. All mapped buildings and canopies remain blockers.
Sun-hours resultDirect clear-sky hours accumulated per ground zone, roof cell, or façade cell over the selected window.
What it shows

Instantaneous shadows use complete building-footprint geometry and are regenerated only when Generate shadows is clicked; orbiting the camera reuses the cached GPU depth texture. The cumulative study places analysis cells on roofs and outward-facing façades, casts a clear-sky ray toward every sampled sun position over an editable daily window (15/30/60-minute steps), and tests it against building prisms and canopy volumes. Full-CBD grids are available at 20 m (faster, ~25,000 cells for an all-surface study) and 10 m (detailed).

Data & method
  • Ground sunlight accumulates from mapped shadow overlap, while roof and façade cells are tested with clear-sky rays against building and canopy geometry.
  • The method focuses on direct sunlight and shadow duration, creating a consistent basis for comparing public spaces and building surfaces.
Designed for early design decisions. Use the results to compare shade, solar access and seasonal performance before advancing preferred options to a detailed daylight or energy assessment.

Wind analysis

Planning analysis

Resolved OpenFOAM airflow for the directions that have a solved case, plus a comfort view weighted by real ERA5 wind-rose frequency.

Quick workflow
  1. Pick a solved wind direction (compass presets light up once a direction has a converted OpenFOAM case) and load the result.
  2. Switch between the Pedestrian, Flow, Slice and Façades diagnostic views.
  3. In Flow, drag the seed box directly in the scene (or use its handle to resize) and set its height; in Slice, drag the plane directly in the scene.
  4. Switch to Comfort to combine every solved direction, weighted by its ERA5 wind-rose frequency.
Controls and outputs
ControlMeaning
DirectionLoads the solved OpenFOAM volume for the picked compass direction; unsolved directions stay disabled until a case is generated for them.
Pedestrian / Flow / Slice / FaçadesTerrain-following speed at 1.5/2/4 m; seeded 3D flowlines in a movable box; a draggable speed/pressure/turbulence slice plane; kinematic pressure on building façades.
Flow regionA resizable box (drag it, or its handle) that bounds where flowlines seed; its height and ground-only-vs-full-height seeding are both adjustable.
ComfortWeights every solved direction's pedestrian field by ERA5 frequency and Weibull exceedance; reports the actual annual-hours coverage achieved rather than assuming full coverage.
What it shows

Direction exposes a full OpenFOAM result per solved compass direction: terrain-following pedestrian speed, 3D velocity, kinematic pressure and turbulence for every mapped building in the solved domain. Comfort combines whichever directions are solved today, weighted by ERA5 wind-rose frequency — it never interpolates a direction that hasn't actually been solved, so its coverage percentage is an honest lower bound, not a full annual study.

Data & method
  • Each solved direction is a steady RANS k-epsilon OpenFOAM case over the full mapped CBD, with a uniform neutral inlet — exploratory, not yet validated against pedestrian-level observations.
  • ERA5 reanalysis supplies each sector's observed frequency and Weibull speed distribution for the Comfort weighting.
  • Mapped building geometry is meshed directly into the solved domain; tree canopy is not yet represented in the CFD mesh.
Built for option comparison. Use the flow and pressure patterns to locate exposed routes, sheltered spaces and areas that benefit from design refinement. Final structural or safety decisions should use a validated, project-specific wind assessment.

Traffic & street closures

Scenario comparison

Draw a lane or street closure directly on the map and compare a paired SUMO simulation against the open-road baseline.

Quick workflow
  1. Choose time period, demand level, junction behaviour and a 5–20 minute duration.
  2. Select Lane, Street, or One-way, then draw over one or more mapped sections.
  3. Confirm the snapped selection, adjust direction where available, and simulate.
  4. Compare Before and With closure, inspect diversion colours, then open the printable report.
Controls and outputs
ControlMeaning
Period and demandSelects a synthetic time-of-day profile at 75%, 100% or 125% of reference demand.
JunctionsUses mapped/generated signal programs or priority right-of-way for the paired runs.
LaneCloses the left-side kerbside lane on selected directional edges, consistent with South African left-hand traffic.
Street / One-wayCloses full selected edges, or the opposite-direction sibling to screen a one-way conversion.
ComparisonJourney time, delay, speed, completion, queue, diversion, emissions, fuel and relative edge-noise differences.
Quality checksComparison metrics are shown only when the baseline, run completion and paired-trip sample meet the model's reliability thresholds.
What it shows

The freehand pen snaps to the SUMO road network and can close one lane or the whole street; in lane mode each selected directional section closes its left-side kerbside lane (South African left-hand traffic). The panel replays baseline and closure traffic, compares mapped signal programs with priority right-of-way, and reports queue, route-length, completion, speed, delay, and road-level diversion metrics. A representative car / minibus-taxi / delivery-van / shuttle fleet carries HBEFA3 emission classes for CO₂, NOx, exhaust PMx, fuel use, and relative edge-noise comparisons. Completed comparisons can generate a print-ready A4 report.

Data & method
  • The reference load is stability-tuned at 50 corridor departures per minute, with consistent demand used in the before-and-after runs.
  • Built-in quality checks protect the comparison from incomplete baselines, timed-out runs and undersized paired samples.
  • Nearby parking and pedestrian crossings are included as useful street context alongside the modelled vehicle network.
Purpose-built for comparing interventions. Use paired scenarios to understand direction and relative scale of network effects, identify diversion routes and refine staging options. Confirm selected schemes with observed counts and operational analysis before implementation.

Public transport

Schedule-based planning

Timetable-derived MyCiTi buses and Metrorail trains, network routes, and an event-access planner.

Quick workflow
  1. Choose Network, Bus or Rail and select a route or corridor.
  2. Scrub or play model time; click a stop or station for scheduled service details.
  3. For an event, set its date, attendance and start/end times.
  4. Select a hub or pick a venue on the map, set walking and service-window assumptions, then run access analysis.
Controls and outputs
ControlMeaning
Model timeInterpolates vehicles along mapped route shapes from supplied timetable endpoints. Playback can run at ¼×, 1× or 5×.
Lines and stopsFilters the network map; clicking a stop shows matched routes and upcoming modelled departures.
Event demandAttendance multiplied by the selected public-transport mode share to create a transparent planning scenario.
Walk shed400, 800 or 1,200 m routed over the mapped walking/street network at 4.8 km/h.
Service windowsCounts arrivals before the event and return departures during the selected dispersal period.
Access resultConnected origin areas, arrival/return counts, scheduled-capacity coverage proxy, service list, grade and ranked interventions.
What it shows

Adds 3D vehicles positioned from schedule interpolation, bold network-map routes, a neutral rail track layer, clickable stops/stations, an accelerated service clock, and event-access planning. Set an event venue from the hub list or by clicking the map; with a date, time window, walking catchment, dispersal window, attendance and mode share, the panel reports connected origin areas, arrival/return service counts, how much of an event-demand proxy the return services' scheduled capacity covers, and ranked corridor interventions.

Data & method
  • PRASA and MyCiTi schedules provide weekday and weekend service patterns along matched routes and corridors.
  • Scheduled capacity uses clear per-departure assumptions of 60 places for a bus and 800 for a train.
  • Walking access follows the mapped pedestrian and street network, including a public-entrance route for Cape Town Station.
A clear view of scheduled access. Vehicle movement represents timetable-based service rather than live tracking. Use the event analysis to identify strong connections, return-service gaps and priority conversations with transport operators.

City model & terrain

LiDAR-grounded

The hybrid terrain, building and roof model every other panel is built on.

What it is

The city model combines 2025 LiDAR terrain, locally aligned SRTM coverage, OpenStreetMap footprints, municipal photogrammetry, street information and mapped tree canopy. Building footprints are fitted to available elevation and roof evidence to create flat, sloped, gable and hip forms, while every layer is aligned in the same projected coordinate system.

Data & method
  • The scene follows the valid terrain footprint, keeping buildings, streets and analysis layers inside mapped ground coverage.
  • Objects retain stable identifiers, source attribution, level of detail and geometry-quality information.
  • Municipal centrelines, lights, crossings, parking and festoon lighting enrich the street-level model.
A consistent spatial foundation. The model is designed to keep terrain, buildings, vegetation, streets, transit and analysis results aligned, making it possible to compare different placemaking questions in one coherent view.

Using results responsibly

Climate Explorer is built to move from city-scale evidence to focused, practical action.

Compare

Use a consistent baseline and change one design, time, weather or mobility assumption at a time.

Prioritise

Identify the streets, public spaces and connections where intervention can have the clearest benefit.

Coordinate

Bring environmental and mobility findings into the same conversation with designers, planners and operators.

Advance

Use the selected priorities to guide site visits, stakeholder engagement and the appropriate detailed technical work.

Clear scope, confident decisions. The tools are strongest for comparing options, locating priorities and building a shared evidence base. Where a decision requires statutory approval, safety certification or detailed engineering, carry the preferred option into the relevant specialist assessment.
